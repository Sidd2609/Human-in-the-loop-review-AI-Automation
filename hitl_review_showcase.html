The problem

Can humans check every AI output?

Fully automated AI at scale will make errors, and people cannot review everything. Review everything and you create a bottleneck; ask too much of reviewers and they become passive approvers. The aim is to spend a fixed review budget on the outputs that carry the most risk.

How outputs are shortlisted

Three layers decide who gets a human

Layer 1 hard gates ignore confidence entirely. Layer 2 scores what is left on likelihood of error times impact. Layer 3 pulls a random sample of what was auto-approved. Counts are per day and follow the simulator inputs below.

    The reviewer experience

    Evidence, not a verdict — and a decision, not a click

    Held items arrive tagged High, Medium or Low so reviewers can prioritise. They see why the item was flagged and the supporting evidence, with no AI approve/reject recommendation. Edit and reject need a reason. Try approving several items quickly.

    The kill switch

    When the world changes, scheduled content must stop

    An external event can make scheduled content inappropriate. An authorised user suppresses a campaign (or all campaigns); its scheduled sends are held at once, owners are told, and on lift the held outputs are re-scored rather than sent.
